Nexus911 01 · Problem · Goal

One emergency, dozens of calls, and no way to tell which one is new.

When dozens of people witness a crime or a crash, every caller is handled as a fresh emergency. Operators collect the same details again and again, conflicting accounts can't be weighed, and a new incident a block away waits behind the tenth duplicate.

Why Massachusetts makes it harder

  • Fragmented dispatch: calls are transferred between centers (highway calls go to State Police), and context is lost at each handoff.
  • Duplicate street names: Washington St and Massachusetts Ave exist in many towns.
  • Two exit-number systems: “old Exit 8” and “Exit 63” are the same place on the Pike.
  • Many languages and channels: Spanish, Portuguese and more, plus short text-to-911 messages.

The goal

  • Link calls about the same event into one incident, and explain why.
  • Raise calls that bring new information (a weapon, an injury, a new place) and lower pure repeats.
  • Weigh conflicting accounts by reliability, recency and independence, not by raw counts.
  • Give officers one trustworthy picture instead of a stream of repeats.
No call is ever dropped.

Nexus911 reorders and annotates the queue; it never hides a call. AI suggests, people decide, and every decision is logged.

Nexus911 02 · How it works · Features

Every call becomes quote-grounded claims in a knowledge graph.

Claude only extracts facts and writes short text. Linking, weighting, priority and dispatch are plain Python rules: testable, explainable and free to run. Two LangGraph graphs run the steps.

01Extract

Claude turns the transcript into claims, each with a verbatim quote.

02Verify

A quote not found in the transcript is rejected.

03Locate

MA gazetteer, old and new exits, landmarks, device location.

04Link

P(same incident) → auto, suggest or new.

05Weigh

Consensus per detail; contradictions kept.

06Prioritize

Severity × novelty, with hard overrides.

07Dispatch

Units, agencies and suggested leads.

Nexus911 03 · Novelties · Results

What's new, and how well it works.

01

Claim-level graph. Every statement is its own node with its caller and quote, so “7 say white, 3 say black” is kept and weighed, not overwritten.

02

Quote-grounded guard. An AI fact without a verbatim span of the transcript is rejected; partial matches stay pending.

03

Information-gain priority. severity × (1 − P_dup × (1 − novelty)): repeats sink, new facts rise.

04

Independence-aware weighting. Hearsay and repeat callers count less, so an echoed rumor can't outvote eyewitnesses.

05

Explainable three-band linking. Auto, suggest or new, with every score visible and one-click Confirm or Split.

06

Massachusetts-aware. Town disambiguation, old and new exits, MBTA, campus and State Police routing, transfers, many languages.
